A Fierce Advocate for California Workers

Attorney Kasra Ramez is the founder and managing attorney of Kas the Lionheart Law, an employment law firm dedicated exclusively to representing employees throughout California. With a reputation for tenacious advocacy and an unwavering commitment to justice, Kasra has built his career on the belief that every working person deserves powerful legal protection — regardless of the size or resources of their employer.

Kasra's path to employment law was shaped by a deep personal conviction that workplace injustice is not merely a legal problem — it is a human one. After witnessing firsthand how employers could exploit workers who lacked the means to fight back, he dedicated himself to becoming the kind of lawyer that corporations fear and employees trust.

Over the course of his legal career, Kasra has represented hundreds of clients in a broad range of employment disputes, from straightforward wage theft claims to complex multi-party discrimination and harassment cases. He approaches every matter — regardless of its size — with the same level of intensity, preparation, and personal attention.

What sets Kasra apart is his refusal to treat clients as case numbers. He takes the time to truly understand each client's story, the impact the employer's actions have had on their livelihood and well-being, and what a truly just outcome looks like for them. That personal approach, combined with a thorough mastery of California and federal employment law, has produced outstanding results for clients across Los Angeles and beyond.

Philosophy

"Your employer has an entire legal department. You deserve someone equally determined, equally skilled, and entirely in your corner. That is what Kas the Lionheart Law is built to provide." — Kasra Ramez

Kasra believes that the best outcomes come from thorough preparation, honest communication, and an absolute refusal to back down when the law is on his client's side. He takes cases on a contingency basis where appropriate, meaning that clients pay nothing unless he wins — because access to justice should not be limited by financial circumstances.
